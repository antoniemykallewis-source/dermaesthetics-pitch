03 / What we found + what we would fix

MAKE IT EASIER
TO CHOOSE YOU.One issue. One clear fix.

Each finding below explains what we saw, why it could get in a customer’s way, and the practical change we would make.

Finding 01 / 06

One page gives patients two addresses.

What we found

The expanded homepage FAQ lists Foothills Boulevard. The footer lists East Roseville Parkway. Both addresses were visible during the same review.

Why this can lose inquiries

Someone planning a first visit has to work out which address is current. That uncertainty can delay a booking or create an avoidable call to the clinic.

Our fix

Confirm the current patient location, then update the FAQ, footer, booking service, Google Business Profile and important local listings so they agree.

Proof: Live FAQ and footer ↗
Finding 02 / 06

Patients cannot clearly meet the provider.

What we found

The homepage refers to licensed specialists and nurses, but its About and Expertise sections do not name a clinician.

Why this can lose inquiries

For a personal treatment, people want to know who will care for them. Unnamed expertise gives them less to connect with before booking.

Our fix

Introduce the provider with their verified role, credentials and an authentic portrait. Film a short, plain-language introduction and consultation explanation for the website and local Meta ads.

Proof: Homepage About / Expertise ↗
Finding 03 / 06

The site leaves its search summary to Google.

What we found

The homepage has a title but no meta description: the short summary a website can suggest for a search result. Google may choose its own summary even when one is supplied.

Why this can lose inquiries

A searcher comparing local clinics may see a less useful introduction instead of a clear explanation of your services and location.

Our fix

Write a concise service-and-location summary, keep business details consistent and support key treatments with useful local pages and blog answers. Check the actual search appearance after updates.

Proof: Fetched homepage metadata ↗
Finding 04 / 06

Google sends both website and booking clicks to Vagaro.

What we found

The Google profile’s website and booking buttons both lead to Vagaro. On the clinic website, the treatment buttons also share one general services-listing destination.

Why this can lose inquiries

Someone who wants to learn about a treatment or provider can land in a booking list too soon. They may need to search again for the information that would make them comfortable choosing.

Our fix

Use the clinic website for Google’s website button and keep Vagaro for booking. Where supported, link a treatment directly to its booking option and explain when a consultation should come first.

Proof: Google website / booking links ↗
Finding 05 / 06

The review total needs a clear source.

What we found

The homepage displays a combined five-star-review total without a nearby breakdown of the platforms or the date it was counted.

Why this can lose inquiries

Interested patients cannot easily open the original reviews or understand what the total covers. That makes strong social proof harder to check.

Our fix

Link to the original profiles and label the source and date. Add permission-cleared patient stories with enough treatment context to make the proof useful.

Proof: Homepage review statistic ↗
Finding 06 / 06

The main mobile screen could load sooner.

What we found

Google’s October 20 mobile lab test scored 67/100. The largest visible element appeared after 8.5 seconds in the simulated test. No real-user dataset was available, so this is not a statement about every visit.

Why this can lose inquiries

Someone arriving from a local ad may leave before the page explains the treatment or shows the booking option.

Our fix

Make the main visual load earlier, reduce unnecessary scripts and improve how downloaded files are reused. Keep booking easy to reach and repeat the same mobile test after changes.

Proof: Mobile PageSpeed report ↗
